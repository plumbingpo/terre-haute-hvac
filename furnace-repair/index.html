From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Terre Haute homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Terre Haute HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve slips past. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, catching airflow problems before they shorten the life of the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. What you get is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
